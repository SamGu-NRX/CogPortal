# Talking with Joel

A coffee chat, not a pitch. Five slides, mostly pictures, and they sit behind
the conversation rather than driving it. The point of the meeting is his read:
where this fits at BWSI, what has been tried before, and who else should be in
the room. After the deck, the demo does the proving.

The three things this is for, in Sam's order:

1. Cultivate more creativity.
2. Make the on-ramp into a project smoother.
3. Get students used to rigorous, industry-standard practice, starting with
   Git, which most of them took as a prerequisite and have never used in anger.

Underneath all three: cover more content with more rigor, while making it
easier and more fun, by removing friction.

## Slide 1: a lunch break in July

On screen: **July 24**, and a clock.

Say: Reynaldo gave up a lunch break to build a demo-day benchmark by hand. He
went to the COCO site, pulled captions at three levels of difficulty, added one
that was not in the dataset at all, and randomized which team got which,
because he did not want anyone optimizing once they had seen them. Then he ran
every team live on the projector.

## Slide 2: half of it failed

On screen: **"See, the vibes are not great."** and, small, "Good job, team.
Clap them up, y'all."

Visuals: a projector screen.

Say: half of it failed. One team got one caption out of three, and a student
called it out loud, and everyone laughed, and Reynaldo closed with clap them
up. That was the best hour of the summer. There was a real unknown in the room,
it was competitive without anyone being ranked against their friends, and
failing in front of everyone was completely normal. What I built is that hour,
made repeatable, so it does not depend on an instructor giving up lunch.

## Slide 3: three things it is for

On screen: three icons, three words. **Creativity. On-ramp. Craft.**

Say, one at a time:

- **Creativity.** Teams take the project in different directions, which is the
  point, and it is also what makes them hard to evaluate. So the tool reads
  whatever they wrote. One team called the function that stores a song `add`;
  another called it `store_fingerprints`; a third kept it on an object. It
  finds all of them by running them. Nobody has to rename anything to be
  measured, so the measurement stops pushing everyone toward one shape.
- **The on-ramp.** Right now a student finds out whether their piece works when
  the pieces come together, which is late. This gives them an answer in twenty
  seconds, on their own machine, in the course's vocabulary, before anyone is
  watching.
- **Craft.** Most of them took Git as a prerequisite and have never really used
  it. Here the repository is the unit: the commit is what gets scored, and the
  work shows up as a history. Using Git properly becomes the path through the
  project instead of a chore beside it.

## Slide 4: every course ends the same way

On screen: **Teams of four. One repo. A demo day.** repeated, small, several
times.

Say: this is the part I wanted your read on. Every BWSI course finishes like
this. The benchmark is different for each one and genuinely expensive to build,
but the shape of the team's three weeks is identical everywhere. Staff see the
demo. Nobody sees the three weeks in front of it. And when the best TA
graduates, everything they knew about how a team should split work goes with
them.

## Slide 5: what I would like from you

On screen: **Where does this fit?** and a coffee cup.

Say: I am not asking you to buy anything. You have been here since the
beginning, so what I want is your read: does this belong to CogWorks, or is it
a BWSI thing, has something like it been tried before, and who else should I be
talking to? If it is worth keeping alive past this summer I would like to know
what that would take.

## Then the demo

Terminal first, then the site. Fifteen minutes, and it answers the question

## Notes for delivery

- It is a conversation. Slides advance when the conversation gets there, not
  the other way around.
- The numbers stay off the slides. If he asks how well it works, the demo is
  right there.
- Do not explain the architecture unless asked. He is not being asked to
  approve a design.
- If he asks what it cost the course: nothing in lecture time. A student
  connects a repository and runs three commands.
