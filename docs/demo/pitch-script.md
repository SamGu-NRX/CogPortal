# Talking with Joel

What I say. The slides move when the conversation gets there.

---

[SLIDE 1]

Reynaldo gave up a lunch break to build a demo-day benchmark by hand. He went
to the COCO site and pulled captions at three levels of difficulty, and added
one that was not in the dataset at all. He randomized which team got which,
because he did not want anyone optimizing once they had seen them. Then he ran
every team live on the projector.

[SLIDE 2]

Half of it failed. One team got one caption out of three, and a student called
it out loud, and everyone laughed, and Reynaldo closed with clap them up.

That was the best hour of the summer. There was a real unknown in the room, it
was competitive without anyone being ranked against their friends, and failing
in front of everyone was completely normal.

What I built is that hour, made repeatable, so it does not depend on an
instructor giving up lunch.

[SLIDE 3]

Creativity. Teams take the project in different directions, which is the point,
and it is also what makes them hard to evaluate. So the tool reads whatever
they wrote. One team called the function that stores a song `add`. Another
called it `store_fingerprints`. A third kept it on an object. It finds all of
them by running them. Nobody has to rename anything to be measured, so the
measurement stops pushing everyone toward one shape.

The on-ramp. Right now a student finds out whether their piece works when the
pieces come together, which is late. This gives them an answer in twenty
seconds, on their own machine, in the course's vocabulary, before anyone is
watching.

Craft. Most of them took Git as a prerequisite and have never really used it.
Here the repository is the unit. The commit is what gets scored, and the work
shows up as a history. Using Git properly becomes the path through the project
instead of a chore beside it.

[SLIDE 4]

This is the part I wanted your read on. Every BWSI course finishes like this.
The benchmark is different for each one and genuinely expensive to build, but
the shape of the team's three weeks is identical everywhere.

Staff see the demo. Nobody sees the three weeks in front of it. And when the
best TA graduates, everything they knew about how a team should split work goes
with them.

[SLIDE 5]

I am not asking you to buy anything. You have been here since the beginning, so
what I want is your read. Does this belong to CogWorks, or is it a BWSI thing?
Has something like it been tried before? And who else should I be talking to?

If it is worth keeping alive past this summer I would like to know what that
would take.
