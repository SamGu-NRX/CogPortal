# Elevator pitch

Thirty seconds, spoken. Slides stay closed. Longer version in `pitch-script.md`.

---

On July 24, Reynaldo gave up his lunch break to build a demo-day benchmark by hand. He pulled captions off the COCO site at three levels of difficulty, added one that wasn't in the dataset, randomized who got which so nobody could optimize, and ran every team live on the projector. Half of it failed, and that was fine. It was the best hour of the summer, and it only happened because one instructor skipped lunch.

I built that hour into software. A team points the tool at their repository, it finds their code by running it (whatever they named things), and in about twenty seconds it tells them what their pipeline does and where it loses, in the course's own words. When it can't read the code, it says exactly why, which is what half a real cohort hits first. The commit is what gets scored, so using Git properly becomes the path through the project instead of a chore beside it.

Every BWSI course ends the same way: teams of four, one repo, a demo day. Staff see the demo. Nobody sees the three weeks in front of it. This is an instrument on those three weeks.

What I want from you is your read: does this belong to CogWorks or to BWSI, has it been tried, and who else should be in the room.
